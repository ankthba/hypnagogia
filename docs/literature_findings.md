ANTAGONIST / CONTROL SET (wake-promoting PPL1 dopaminergic dFB tangential neurons): ['FB5H', 'FB6H', 'FB7B'] - exclude these from any "dFB activation" manipulation; they are the cells that switch dFB neurons OFF (Pimentel et al. 2016).

ALTERNATIVE SLEEP-HOMEOSTAT POPULATION: ['ER5'] (21 neurons, GABAergic; = R5 = the "R2" of Liu et al. 2016), with ['ExR1'] (4 neurons) as the helicon-cell relay and ['ExR3'] (2, dopaminergic) as a second EB sleep-wake element. ER5 is attractive as a readout because it has published rate/oscillation phenomenology (~3-fold spontaneous rate increase with sleep pressure; 0.5-1.5 Hz delta-band network oscillation in rested flies), whereas the dFB has none in absolute Hz.

Cite the mapping as: Hulse et al. 2021 eLife 10:e66039, Figure 48 and Figure 48-figure supplements 2-6 (LM-to-EM MCFO matching of R23E10), with FlyWire naming per Schlegel et al. 2024 Nature (flyconnectome/flywire_annotations, 783 release).

Three modelling cautions to carry into the write-up:
1. Neurotransmitter. All nine dFB types are ML-predicted glutamatergic in v783, and Shiu et al.'s LIF model treats glutamate as inhibitory - so an out-of-the-box build makes the "sleep-promoting" dFB an inhibitory population. Jones et al. 2025 report that most dFB neurons co-express VGlut and ChAT, none are GABAergic, and cholinergic dFB cells are the most potent sleep regulators. Run at least one variant with the dFB set excitatory, and report sensitivity to this choice.
2. No published dFB firing rate. There is no absolute spontaneous spike rate for dFB neurons in Donlea 2014 / Pimentel 2016 - only binary ON (spiking, high Rm, long tau_m) vs OFF (silent; Rm at 53% and tau_m at 24% of ON values) and evoked spike-frequency thresholds of 1-50 Hz under current injection. Implement the "sleep state" as a tonic depolarizing drive / raised excitability of the dFB set rather than by clamping it to a literature rate, and calibrate any target rate against the ExR1 (helicon) UP-state value of 16.9 +/- 3.6 Hz, which is the only well-defined number in this circuit.
3. The 23E10 controversy does not threaten the EM identity of these cells, only the behavioural attribution. Because FAFB is brain-only, the VNC-SP confound cannot be reproduced or ruled out in this model - state that explicitly, and frame the dFB manipulation as "activating the connectome-defined dFB tangential types", not "activating 23E10".

# TOPIC: kc-mbon-plasticity

## Recommendation
Use a TWO-factor, dopamine-gated, anti-Hebbian rule at KC->MBON synapses with no postsynaptic (MBON) term. The primary evidence (Hige 2015, Neuron: LTD of equal size with MBON spikes blocked by QX-314 or voltage clamp) and every published computational rule (Jiang & Litwin-Kumar 2021 Eq 4; Eschbach 2020 Eq 2; Springer & Nawrot 2021 Eq 7; Bennett 2021 has no direct MBON term at the synapse) agree on this; a true three-factor KC x MBON x DAN rule is NOT literature-supported. MBON activity influences plasticity only indirectly via MBON->DAN feedback (Zhao 2021, Bennett 2021), which the FlyWire connectome already provides if DANs are simulated.

Concrete Brian2 rule (per KC->MBON synapse j->i, DAN = dopaminergic neurons innervating MBON i's compartment):
  state variables: x_KC (presynaptic eligibility trace), x_DA (dopamine trace), w in [0, w_max]
  dx_KC/dt = -x_KC/tau_KC       ; on KC spike: x_KC += 1
  dx_DA/dt = -x_DA/tau_DA       ; on DAN spike: x_DA += 1
  on DAN spike (depression, forward pairing, DopR1/cAMP arm): w -= eta_LTD * x_KC
  on KC spike  (potentiation, backward pairing, DopR2/ER-Ca arm): w += eta_LTP * x_DA
  clip w to [w_min, w_max]; optional nonspecific potentiation: dw/dt += beta * x_DA (Jiang Eq 5) to model unpaired-DAN restoration (Cohn 2015, Berry 2018).
This is the spiking discretization of Jiang & Litwin-Kumar Eq 4, dw/dt = r̄_DAN r_KC - r̄_KC r_DAN (code: stdp_update = -outer(da, sbar) + outer(dabar, s), clipped to [0, wmax]).

Constants with a source:
- Sign/direction: coincident KC+DAN -> depression (Hige 2015: -80% spikes, -90% EPSC charge after ONE 1-s pairing with 4 DAN light pulses in γ1pedc; Cohn 2015 γ4; Berry 2018 γ2α'1). Calibrate eta_LTD so that one 1-s odor + 4-spike-burst DAN episode drives the paired-KC synapses to ~0.1-0.2 w0 (Hige).
- Temporal window: forward ISI 0 to +0.5 s -> depression, -1.2 s -> potentiation, +6 s -> ~none (Handler 2019 Fig 2E); Hige: DAN 0.2 s after odor onset -> LTD, odor 0.5 s after DAN -> nothing. Jiang/Eschbach use tau_KC = tau_DA = 5 s (Jiang code definemodel.py: tau = 5). Zhao 2021 fits a KC eligibility trace tau_o ~ 10-15 s from trace conditioning behavior. Recommend tau_KC = 5 s (Jiang) as default, with 1-2 s as a Handler-consistent alternative to test.
- Weight bounds: w in [0, w_max], w initialized at w_max (Jiang: w_max = 1/(N_KC f) = 0.05; Eschbach: 1/(N_KC f)). Given Hige's 90% max EPSC reduction, w_min = 0.1 w0 is a defensible floor; w_min = 0 per models.
- Induction smoothing: tau_W = 5 s (Jiang: 'timescale of the induction of LTD or LTP'; Eschbach Eq 3).
- Persistence: no decay term needed on a minutes-long sleep simulation (Hige: >=40 min with little recovery; Berry: stable >=15 min; Handler: 'decayed slowly').
- Nonspecific potentiation by DAN alone: beta initialized 0.01, compartment-specific, positive (Jiang Eq 5); qualitatively required by Cohn 2015/Berry 2018. Important for a sleep simulation because Cohn 2015 shows state-dependent spontaneous DAN activity (quiescence -> high γ4/γ5, low γ2/γ3 DANs), so baseline DAN firing during 'sleep' will slowly erase (potentiate) a depressed engram unless beta is small or DAN baseline is silenced.
- Synapse counts / initial weights per KC->MBON pair: take per-synapse counts directly from FlyWire v783 (Takemura 2017 α-lobe reference: ~10-14 synapses per connected KC per MBON; Li 2020: 122-1694 KCs per MBON); scale w0 so that the initial MBON odor response is realistic (Hige: MBON-γ1pedc ~84 Hz during odor, EPSC >200 pA).
- Compartment specificity: apply the rule only between DANs and MBONs sharing a compartment (Aso 2014a: 15 compartments; Hige 2015: no spillover to adjacent compartment), and allow a compartment-specific eta (γ1pedc needs 1 s, α2 needs ~1 min pairing; Hige 2015).

Constants that must be FREE (no direct citation): eta_LTD per DAN spike (only calibratable to the Hige 80-90% single-pairing endpoint, given the unknown number of PPL1 spikes evoked by 4 light pulses); eta_LTP/eta_LTD ratio (Handler shows potentiation is comparable in size but gives no number); tau_DA (5 s in Jiang code, 'few hundred ms' order sensitivity from Handler/Hige suggests shorter); w_min (0 vs 0.1 w0); beta (0.01 in Jiang is an optimization initial value, not a measurement); the spike-to-rate conversion of the rate rules; per-compartment eta scaling; whether DopR2 potentiation exists in the specific compartment used (Hige saw no backward-pairing effect in γ1pedc, Handler saw it in γ2/γ4/γ5).

## Open questions
- Jiang & Litwin-Kumar's 5-s eligibility trace (code only; not stated numerically in the paper) is longer than Handler 2019's window (minimal plasticity at ISI +6 s, valence switch with a few-hundred-ms shift) and Hige 2015's sub-second order sensitivity imply; the correct tau_KC for a spiking model (1-2 s vs 5 s vs Zhao's ~10-15 s behavioral trace) is unresolved and should be a sensitivity parameter.
- Numeric magnitudes of depression/potentiation in Handler 2019 (γ4 MBON) and Berry 2018 (γ2α'1) are only shown graphically; only Hige 2015 gives numbers (80 ± 5.7% spikes, 90 ± 3.7% EPSC charge, single 1-s pairing, γ1pedc).
- The number of PPL1-γ1pedc spikes evoked by Hige's 4 x 1-ms light pulses (Fig S2) was not extracted; eta_LTD per DAN spike therefore cannot be pinned exactly.
- Eschbach 2020's numeric tau and tau_W values were not found in the PMC Methods text (may be in the code/supplement).
- Modi, Shuai & Turner 2020 (Annu Rev Neurosci) could not be accessed (paywalled) and was not read.
- Whether backward-pairing potentiation (DopR2 arm) exists in γ1pedc: Hige 2015 saw no effect of backward pairing there, whereas Handler 2019 saw potentiation in γ2, γ4, γ5 with PAM/PPL DANs — compartment dependence of the LTP arm is unclear.
- How strongly spontaneous/state-dependent DAN activity (Cohn 2015: quiescence -> high γ4/γ5 DAN activity) would potentiate and thus erase a depressed KC->MBON engram over a minutes-long simulated sleep period is not quantified in any source; the beta (nonspecific potentiation) parameter is unconstrained.
- Per-KC->MBON synapse counts should be recomputed from FlyWire v783; the only published per-KC numbers (Takemura 2017, ~10-14 synapses per connected KC per MBON) are from the α lobe of a different EM volume.

# TOPIC: criticality-methods

## Recommendation
Implement three independent criticality measures and report all of them per simulation, never a single one. (A) Branching ratio via MR estimator (mrestimator, Spitzner 2021 / Wilting & Priesemann 2018): bin the whole-population spike count at Δt ≈ 1–4 ms (Wilting used 4 ms; note m depends on Δt but τ does not), cut the run into equal-length trials each ≥ 10·τ_estimate, call mre.coefficients(data, method='stationarymean' (or 'ts' as consistency check), steps=(1, kmax), dt=Δt, dtunit='ms', numboot=100) with kmax spanning several autocorrelation times (start with kmax≈500–1000 steps, then set kmax ≈ 5–10 τ/Δt), then mre.fit(rk, fitfunc='exp_offset') (use 'complex' if oscillations are present); report m = fit.mre, τ = fit.tau = −Δt/ln(m), and the bootstrap CI. Also compute the naive one-step slope r_1 (= m̂_C) and Priesemann's σ* = mean over bins of spikes(bin i)/spikes(bin i−1); in a fully sampled simulation r_1 ≈ m, but σ* is bin-size dependent and biased, so treat it only as a sanity check. Classify with published thresholds: m ≤ 0.5 input-driven/irregular; 0.5 < m < 1 reverberating (in vivo cortex ≈ 0.98, ε = 1 − m ≈ 0.02); m ≈ 1 (within bootstrap CI) near-critical; m > 1 supercritical/bursting. (B) Avalanches: bin population spikes with Δt = ⟨IEI⟩ = 1/(population rate) (Beggs & Plenz 2003; Priesemann 2014; Fontenele 2019) and also at 0.5× and 2× that to check robustness; an avalanche = maximal run of consecutive non-empty bins bounded by empty bins; size S = total spikes, duration T = number of bins. Fit with powerlaw.Fit(sizes, discrete=True) (and durations likewise); let xmin be chosen by KS minimization (or fix xmin=1 and xmax = e.g. 100 as in Fontenele if you want comparability); require ≥ 50 avalanches for parameter estimates and ideally ≥ 1000 in the tail for xmin (Clauset 2009). Run fit.distribution_compare('power_law','exponential'), ('power_law','lognormal'), ('power_law','truncated_power_law', nested=True); interpret: R>0 favors the power law, R<0 the alternative, and only trust the sign if p < 0.1 (Clauset) / p < 0.05 (powerlaw docstring); expect the lognormal to often win in a driven network (Priesemann 2014). Report τ (size exponent), α (duration exponent), fit.power_law.sigma, and the number of avalanches. (C) Crackling relation and shape collapse (Friedman 2012, Fontenele 2019): compute ⟨S⟩(T) ~ T^{1/(σνz)} by regression, compare with (α−1)/(τ−1); then collapse mean profiles s(t,T)·T^{1−1/(σνz)} vs t/T for durations with enough avalanches (e.g. ≥ 20 per T) and quantify collapse error; expect MF-DP values τ=1.5, α=2, 1/(σνz)=2 for a critical branching process, or empirical τ≈1.5–1.6, α≈1.7, 1/(σνz)≈1.3. Always include shuffled-spike surrogates (which must not collapse and should lose power-law support) because non-critical driven systems can pass power-law tests (Destexhe & Touboul 2021; Fontenele's own caveat). Define 'silent' and 'saturated' operationally in the pipeline (e.g., silent: population rate below a floor so that avalanches/MR are undefined; saturated: m > 1 with a hump at S ≈ N and near-constant activity), since no paper supplies those exact labels.

# TOPIC: replay-metrics

## Recommendation
Implement three complementary, well-controlled metrics in the Brian2 pipeline and frame the fly result as a model prediction, because no offline KC/MBON reactivation has ever been observed in Drosophila (only DAN-aSP13 'reactivation' read out over hours by a transcriptional reporter, Dag 2019). (1) Pairwise/EV-REV (Kudrimoti 1999; Tingley & Peyrache 2020): bin all KC (and MBON) spike trains at 100 ms (also run 50 and 200 ms), compute the Pearson correlation for every cell pair in PRE-sleep, ODOR/TRAINING and POST-sleep epochs, then EV = squared partial correlation of the RUN-POST pair-correlation vectors given PRE, and REV with PRE/POST swapped; require EV > REV and EV_POST > EV_PRE. (2) Assembly reactivation strength (Peyrache 2009/2010; Lopes-dos-Santos 2013): z-score per-neuron spike counts from the training epoch, correlation matrix C = ZZ^T/N_bins, keep eigenvectors above the Marcenko-Pastur bound lambda_max = (1 + sqrt(N_neurons/N_bins))^2 (optionally ICA-rotate), build P = w w^T with diag(P)=0, and track R(t) = z_sleep(t)^T P z_sleep(t) in POST vs PRE sleep using the tortlab assembly_activity.m logic; also define the 'known' learned KC ensemble directly (odor-responsive KCs) as an explicit template w. (3) Sequence/rank-order test only if the model produces bursts: per candidate event use Spearman rank-order correlation between template order and spike time with >=100 cell-order shuffles (Foster & Wilson 2006), 10-25 ms bins for any decoding, and report the proportion of significant events against a 5-20% chance floor (Tingley & Peyrache). Null models to include: per-neuron circular time shifts and bin shuffles (Lopes-dos-Santos), cell-identity shuffles (Nadasdy 1999), size-matched random KC ensembles drawn from the same rate distribution (to address the firing-rate confound emphasized by Tatsuno 2006 and Tingley & Peyrache 2020), and PRE-sleep as the internal control. Bin choice: KCs fire ~0.1 Hz spontaneously and respond in ~200 ms bursts (Turner 2008), so use 50-200 ms bins for reactivation (100 ms default) and 10-25 ms only for within-burst sequence analysis; with sparse spiking, ensure N_bins >> N_neurons (q large) so the Marcenko-Pastur bound is valid, and check for rate-driven artefacts. Report EV/REV, R(t) POST-vs-PRE, and fraction of significant events with their null distributions; do not treat the '25 ms' bin as a literature-fixed value since it was not found in Lopes-dos-Santos 2013.

# TOPIC: brian2-technical

## Recommendation
Pipeline design for the 138k-neuron / 15M-synapse standalone sweep on this Mac (Brian2 2.10.1):

1. One build, many runs. set_device('cpp_standalone', build_on_run=False); build the model once with run(T); then device.build(run=False, directory='<abs path>') (pass directory explicitly; build_options from set_device are ignored by a manual build). Iterate conditions and seeds with device.run(run_args={...}, results_directory=f'results_{cond}_{seed}', with_output=False).

2. Parameters you want to sweep must be model variables, not namespace constants: declare e.g. 'tau_m : second (shared, constant)', 'sigma : volt (shared, constant)', per-neuron arrays such as 'v0 : volt (constant)', and pass G.tau_m / G.sigma / G.v0 / S.w / P.rates (PoissonGroup rates array) / a TimedArray in run_args. Namespace constants are compiled in as C++ const literals.

3. Seeds: seed() is compiled into main.cpp and there is no device.run(seed=...) or run_args seed. Do NOT call seed(); instead device.insert_code('after_start', <getenv("BRIAN_SEED") loop seeding brian::_random_generators[i] with base+i>) before build, and set prefs.devices.cpp_standalone.run_environment_variables['BRIAN_SEED']=str(seed) before each device.run(). Verified reproducible and distinct on this machine. If you also draw connectivity/initial values in numpy, seed numpy separately.

4. Noise: write 'dv/dt = (v0 - v)/tau_m + sigma*sqrt(2/tau_m)*xi : volt (unless refractory)' with method='euler' (explicitly; 'linear'/'exact' refuse xi). Stationary std is sigma (Euler bias factor 1/sqrt(1-dt/(2 tau)) ~ 0.1% at dt=0.1 ms, tau=20 ms). The refractory flag multiplies the noise term by int(not_refractory) as well - that is fine and expected.

5. Input: PoissonInput works in standalone (including on subgroups), and hundreds of them are fine at run time but they are not changeable via run_args (rate baked into the binomial) and each adds a code object/compile unit. If rates must vary per condition, use one PoissonGroup(N, rates=array) + Synapses and pass P.rates in run_args, or move the rate into a per-neuron parameter of the target group and add the noise-like input directly in the equations.

6. Plasticity: use the Izhikevich_2007 pattern - plastic Synapses with (event-driven) Apre/Apost, per-synapse eligibility c, and gate with dopamine; to save ~120-240 MB per per-synapse variable on 15M synapses, keep the dopamine trace on the postsynaptic neuron (DAN->KC/MBON Synapses on_pre='da_post += eps', 'dda/dt=-da/taud : 1' in the neuron) and use 'dw/dt = c*da_post/taus : 1 (clock-driven)', or apply w updates only in on_pre/on_post. Budget ~0.2 GB for indices plus ~0.12 GB per double synaptic variable; give the scalar delay in the Synapses constructor (delay=1.8*ms) so it is not stored per synapse.

7. Compiler/OpenMP: Apple clang cannot take -fopenmp. To use OpenMP set os.environ['CXX']='g++-16' AND os.environ['SDKROOT']=xcrun --show-sdk-path (homebrew gcc's configured sysroot is missing on this OS) before device.build, then prefs.devices.cpp_standalone.openmp_threads=N. Brian warns OpenMP is not well tested - validate against a 1-thread run and benchmark; results are reproducible for a fixed thread count.

8. Monitors: SpikeMonitor costs 12 B/spike, so recording all 138k neurons is affordable (tens of MB per run); use record=False (counts only) or SpikeMonitor(G[a:b]) subgroups plus PopulationRateMonitor when only rates are needed; copy mon.i[:]/mon.t[:] (or read the results_<...>/ files) after each device.run since results are overwritten per results_directory.

9. Multiple structurally different networks in one process: device.reinit(); device.activate(build_on_run=False, directory=...); defaultclock.dt = 0.1*ms; build inside a function or with explicit Network objects (stale magic-network objects raise MagicError); pass directory= to device.build. Separate directories only needed to keep both builds or run them concurrently.

## Open questions
- Actual peak RSS for the 15.09M-synapse network was not measured; the ~0.2 GB (indices) + 0.12 GB per double per-synapse variable figure is derived from the storage layout, and synapse creation (connect with i/j arrays) transiently needs additional copies.
- OpenMP speed-up for this network shape (sparse, delayed, with clock-driven synaptic equations) was not benchmarked; Brian2 itself warns OpenMP is 'not yet well tested'. A 1- vs 4-thread validation run on the real model is advisable.
- The env-var seeding recipe relies on internal symbols (brian::_random_generators, RandomGenerator::seed) of the 2.10.1 templates; it is verified here but not a documented API and may need updating in future Brian versions.
- Whether the run_args static-array mechanism (writing init_*.dat files of 138k doubles per run) is fast enough for hundreds of runs was not timed; it is a file write plus binary load per run and should be negligible next to simulation time.

# TOPIC: fly-olfactory-drive-and-sleep-state

## Recommendation
Input-side parameters for the whole-brain LIF model, grounded in the sources above:

(a) ODOR STIMULATION. Drive ORNs as Poisson processes. Spontaneous: 1-15 Hz depending on ORN class (de Bruyne 2001); use ~5-8 Hz class-average if a single value is needed. Odor-evoked: pick a monomolecular odor that at 10^-2 dilution excites ~4 of the ~24 OR types at >=50 Hz, ~2 at >=100 Hz, and at most 1 at >=200 Hz (Hallem & Carlson 2006 statistics: 17% / 9% / 3% of receptor-odor pairs), with peak rates capped around 200-225 Hz (de Bruyne 2001) and ~11% of ORN types inhibited below baseline. Use a 0.5-1 s pulse (Hallem: 500 ms; Turner/Honegger: 500 ms-1 s) with a fast onset and partial adaptation over 1-2 s. Map OR types to the FlyWire ORN glomerulus annotations. Validate the model against the KC target: 5-6% of KCs spiking (Turner 2008: 6 ± 5%; Honegger 2011: ~5%, never >10% population mean), 2-5 spikes per responding KC per odor (α/β 2.2 ± 1.2; α'/β' 4.9 ± 3.0), KC spontaneous rate ~0.1 Hz, PN spontaneous ~4.6 Hz with ~59% of PNs responding. If the model KC ensemble exceeds ~10%, lower ORN evoked gain or check APL feedback inhibition (Lin 2014).

(b) CONDITIONING / DAN. Two literature-exact protocols: (1) Hige 2015: 1-s odor, four 1-ms CsChrimson pulses at 2 Hz to PPL1-γ1pedc starting 0.2 s after odor onset, producing ~80% depression of MBON-γ1pedc>α/β odor responses (118 -> 24 spikes) — a single pairing suffices; (2) Aso & Rubin 2016: 60-s odor with thirty 1-s light pulses (0.5 Hz) starting 5 s after odor onset; PPL1-γ1pedc gives robust single-trial aversive memory; DAN activation must fall within ~30 s after odor onset for aversive memory (before odor by 20-60 s gives appetitive). Implement DAN 'activation' as forced spiking of PPL1-γ1pedc during those windows; no verified Hz value for shock-evoked PPL1 firing exists, so treat DAN spike rate as a free parameter (calcium responses scale with shock current, Mao & Davis 2009). Apply a KC->MBON-γ1pedc depression rule gated by odor-then-DAN order.

(c) SLEEP STATE. Model two stages: 'active sleep' (first ~5 min of quiescence; near-wake mean activity, reduced connectivity/correlation, sensory responses suppressed — Tainton-Heap 2021) and 'quiet sleep' (>10 min; lowest activity; LFP 11-80 Hz attenuated, ~60% loss of 3-50 Hz LFP power in Nitz 2002 — implemented e.g. as a global reduction of excitatory drive/gain or of background input by ~40-60%). Optionally add a 7-10 Hz modulatory oscillation for early/transitional sleep (Yap 2017). Include the MB sleep circuitry effects: raise activity of γd KCs and MBON-γ2α'1 and reduce α'/β', γ-main KC and glutamatergic MBON-γ5β'2a/β'2mp activity in the sleep condition (Sitaraman 2015; Aso 2014). DAN state: lower PAM and PPL1 baseline activity during sleep (bioRxiv 2025).

(d) ORN BACKGROUND DURING SLEEP: keep it on. No evidence was found that ORN spontaneous spiking stops in sleep; it is peripherally generated, and sleeping flies still discriminate odors with KC odor-identity coding intact. Reduce responsiveness centrally (gain/threshold, connectivity) rather than by silencing ORNs; optionally test a control with ORN background removed to show the reactivation result is not driven by peripheral noise.

## Open questions
- Absolute shock-evoked and spontaneous firing rates (Hz) of PPL1-γ1pedc / MP1 DANs were not verified from any primary electrophysiology source; the iScience 2021 NPF/PPL1 paper (10.1016/j.isci.2021.103506) reportedly contains PPL1 spontaneous rates but was blocked (captcha).
- Hallem & Carlson 2006 receptor-level spontaneous rates (used to convert to absolute rates) were not in the extracted text; values are in their figures and in Hallem, Ho & Carlson 2004 Cell — de Bruyne 2001 values were used instead.
- Tainton-Heap 2021 gives no absolute % of active neurons for the quiet (>10 min) sleep stage in main text; the fractional reduction for 'quiet sleep' has to be inferred (or taken from LFP: ~60% power drop in Nitz 2002, 11-80 Hz attenuation in van Alphen 2013).
- French et al. 2021 Nature (sensory processing during sleep) full text was paywalled; the exact peripheral 'gate-points' (which ORN types / whether ORN activity is reduced during sleep) could not be verified.
- Whether ORN spontaneous spiking itself changes with sleep state has apparently never been measured directly; this remains an assumption of the model.
- The bioRxiv 2025 sleep/DAN paper is a preprint (not peer reviewed); its PAM/PPL1 sleep modulation should be treated as provisional.
